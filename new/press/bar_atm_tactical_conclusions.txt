1. BAR (Barcelona)

BUILD-UP UNDER PRESSURE

Strengths
- BAR rarely panics against a lone presser. Quick circulation and first-time passes usually move the ball off the pressed player before a second opponent arrives.
- Switches of play are a strong weapon. Moving the ball quickly to the far side tends to leave the opposing block compact on the wrong side, and it opens long, unopposed carries for the centre-backs.
- Inside passes that split a lone wide presser from the midfield behind him often break the first line cleanly.
- There is usually a short, free option ahead of the ball. A forward who drops to hold the ball up gives the build-up a reliable target under contact.
- When a long ball is lost, BAR tends to counter-press immediately and win it back with nearby midfielders.

Weaknesses
- The main risk is the touchline. Wide players who keep dribbling along the line instead of releasing early get boxed in, and inside support angles are often missing.
- Central carriers can get caught in tight clusters near halfway, with few forward options. The resulting rushed passes are picked off by the covering defenders.
- Near the sideline, BAR sometimes has teammates level or behind but nobody ahead or inside. This makes misplaced passes more likely, even before real pressure arrives.

PRESSING

Strengths
- The press comes in waves from different players. Carriers rarely get time to settle, because when one presser is beaten, another arrives quickly.
- The touchline is used as an extra defender. Ball-side squeezes with several players closing fast are the most reliable way BAR wins the ball.
- Cover behind the press is usually compact and well placed. A high, ready line and midfielders positioned behind the pressers tend to collect forced long balls and clearances.
- Pressure on the goalkeeper and deep centre-backs often pushes ATM into long balls toward zones BAR already controls.

Weaknesses
- Forwards often press alone, with no second player cutting the return or lateral pass. This lets ATM escape with a simple one-touch combination.
- Pressure tends to be intermittent. Lulls in which ATM carries unpressed often precede ATM's successful progressions.
- Pressers often arrive just after the pass is played, closing the receiver late instead of denying the reception.
- Some pressing wins do not produce possession: the ball goes out of play and ATM restarts.


2. ATM (Atletico Madrid)

BUILD-UP UNDER PRESSURE

Strengths
- When ATM keeps its shape, it can exploit an isolated presser. It uses the spare player at the back, recycles through the centre-backs, and moves the ball from wide to central to pull the press out of position.
- One-touch wall passes through midfield are effective at bypassing central pressers who arrive late.
- Spreading play with the wide players and full-backs can stretch BAR's block enough to open free lanes into the final third.

Weaknesses
- ATM often drifts the ball toward the touchline, sideways and backwards, and walks into BAR's squeezes there.
- Long balls and clearances under even moderate pressure are a recurring problem. From the goalkeeper and centre-backs, they tend to land in zones where BAR's high line and midfield cover are waiting.
- Decision-making is a frequent cause of losses even without heavy pressure:
    - forward passes into already covered teammates
    - passes released just as a presser is closing
    - free short or backward options left unused
- Support near the ball is often thin. Carriers frequently have no teammate close enough for a safe short pass, so long or forced passes become the only exit.
- Players sometimes carry the ball toward the pressure instead of away from it, which compresses their own options.

PRESSING

Strengths
- When ATM commits several players quickly onto an isolated carrier, with a compact block behind, it wins the ball cleanly. This works especially near the touchline or in a central cluster.
- Individual defenders close very fast and can punish a loose pass near the sideline.

Weaknesses
- Pressure is usually single-man, short and late, with no cover or trap behind the presser, so one pass is enough to escape.
- The block often stays compact but deep, with numbers behind the ball rather than on it. Centre-backs are allowed to carry long distances unchallenged.
- ATM struggles to shift across after a switch of play and ends up compact on the wrong side.
- Pressers leave their positions without cover, which splits the block and opens lanes to free receivers between the lines.
